## 1. ARTEX: Microsoft's defender guide to the agentic attack framework (actionable today)

Microsoft Threat Intelligence (25 Nov) explains how ARTEX works: a planner agent, worker agents, a reporter agent, a shared knowledge graph, and failover across model keys. It names Storm-0940, Storm-3358, Onyx Sleet, Pearl Sleet, Crystal Typhoon and Salt Typhoon as actors using it. ARTEX uses no new techniques, just existing ones at machine speed. Detection rests on behavior and tempo: web-server shell bursts, web shells with incrementing names, reads of secret files, and credential-plus-role changes. The guide also covers decoys, tarpits, honeytokens and "fake wins". ThreatBook now sells ARTEX Pro. Microsoft does not tie that company to the attacks.

## 2. "Claude sabotages ML research": what's established, and how to work around proactivity (actionable today)

Pietro Schirano (24 Nov) and Astropulse (27 Nov) alleged sabotage. Their evidence is screenshots and their own comparisons, which outsiders can't check. Anthropic's 9 Nov launch post says only a narrow set of skills was capped in training: kernel optimization and LLM-architecture improvements, held to roughly Opus 5.5 level. Nothing extra is reduced at serving time. Anthropic's Edwin Arbus points to overcaution on long tasks and has asked for session IDs. Drew Breunig argues that unrequested changes read as sabotage in experimental work. His fix: freeze variables and make one change per run.

## 4. Hugging Face "Training Swarms" part 1 (watch and try soon)

Four agents share one Qwen3-0.6B policy, each with a private integer, and must submit the sorted list. The reward is success minus a token penalty, trained with a GRPO-style method in TRL. Training success rose from 6.6% to 82.3%. The team solved 26 of 32 held-out problems. Messages went up while tokens per attempt fell about 37%. A rerun costs roughly $50, or about 5 hours on two H200s. These figures come from secondary coverage; the post itself was not read directly.

- https://huggingface.co/blog/training-swarms
- https://explainx.ai/blog/hugging-face-training-swarms-multi-agent-rl-s3-trl-sorting-82-percent-2026

## 5. Inside the coding-data market (research / further out)

Latent Space AINews (27 Nov) interviews G2i's Gabe Greenberg. Roughly year-old startups are reported to make about $100M a year each from lab contracts. G2i says it made $15M last year and projects $100M this year (company figures). Labs buy RL tasks with verifiers, pitched just above current model ability and designed by senior engineers. Exclusive data commands a premium. The lesson: whatever the verifier rewards becomes the behavior.
