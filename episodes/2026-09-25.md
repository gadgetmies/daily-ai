# AI Round-up, 25 September

A new independent rating system for GPU cloud providers, Amazon opening Seller Central to outside AI agents including Claude, Runway's real-time interactive world model, and Google's search-based approach to scientific research.

## ClusterMAX 3.0: an independent report card for renting GPUs

SemiAnalysis published the third edition of ClusterMAX, its rating system for GPU cloud ("neocloud") providers, scoring more than 300 of them (up from ~209 in v2, ~169 in v1) on compute performance, networking, storage, orchestration, monitoring, support, security, and pricing — tested directly rather than taken on the provider's word. Nebius joined CoreWeave in the top Platinum tier, previously a club of one.

## Amazon opens Seller Central to outside AI agents, starting with Claude

At Amazon Accelerate, Amazon launched a US-beta plugin letting third-party sellers manage inventory, pricing, listings, and analytics via Anthropic's Claude or Amazon's own Quick assistant, instead of the Seller Central dashboard. The Seller Assistant runs on Amazon Bedrock, combining Amazon Nova and Claude, carries persistent cross-surface memory of a seller's pricing patterns and goals, and supports standing autonomous instructions (e.g. "alert me if a top product drops below 4 stars," "watch my category and adjust pricing automatically"). Seller data stays inside Amazon's infrastructure. A free 12-month Amazon Quick Plus subscription is bundled through December 31, 2026.

## Runway's GWM Worlds 2 and the WorldPrompt format

Runway published research on GWM Worlds 2, a real-time interactive "general world model" generating continuous 720p video at 24 fps with synchronized 48kHz audio (speech, effects, ambience), steerable live via a new WorldPrompt input format (fixed elements like the first frame, plus a stream of timestamped actions). Built by fine-tuning Runway's audio-video model to the WorldPrompt format, post-training it to generate autoregressively, then distilling it down to real-time speed. Runway points at interactive entertainment, virtual characters, and robotics/embodied-agent simulation as applications.

## Google's ERA: treating science as a search problem

In a Latent Space interview, Google researcher John Platt described ERA (Empirical Research Assistance), which reframes scientific problems as "scorable tasks" — code or configurations that can be automatically graded — and uses Gemini plus tree search to hunt for higher-scoring versions, guided by Gemini's built-in domain knowledge rather than blind search. The concrete application discussed: contrail mitigation (contrails cause roughly a third of aviation's climate impact), building on Google's six-year North Atlantic Tracks flight-rerouting project.
