# AI round-up, 20 November 2026

Gemini API Tool Retrieval, Perplexity's Orchestrator interface, US banks putting AI-agent losses on customers, RoboBuilder's sim-to-real robot co-design, and why models make new research sound old.

## 1. Gemini API Tool Retrieval (preview) — *actionable today*

Google added Tool Retrieval to the Gemini Interactions API on 19 November. You add a `tool_search` tool and set `defer_loading: true` on the tools you want held back, including whole `mcp_server` tools. The model then searches for the schemas it needs and loads only those. Search runs on Google's servers by default, or in your own app if you set execution to client. Steps appear in `interaction.steps`. It works with Gemini 3.6 and later, including all Gemini 4 models, and there is no extra fee.

Caveats: the server-side search is an extra model call and can cost more than it saves on small libraries. Retrieved schemas are billed as input for the rest of the conversation. Google published no benchmarks. Anthropic's API already has an equivalent tool search tool.

## 2. Perplexity Orchestrator API — *actionable soon*

Launched 17 November. One endpoint and one key cover proprietary models (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, xAI) and open-weight models (for example Kimi, GLM and DeepSeek), which Perplexity hosts itself or serves through partners. It accepts the OpenAI Chat Completions, OpenAI Responses and Anthropic Messages formats, so you only change the base URL (`https://api.perplexity.ai/orchestrator/v1`) and the key. Perplexity says it routes traffic by provider health, capacity and latency, with no platform fee.

Caveats: the docs say private preview, with access by request to api@perplexity.ai, even though the blog says it is launching now. The success-rate claim is Perplexity's own figure.

## 3. US banks put third-party AI-agent losses on customers — *actionable today*

Citi's updated online terms say customers who give an outside agent access are responsible for what is done through it, including what attackers do if the agent leaks credentials or is hacked. Citi's own AI tools are exempt. A Citi transfer agreement reportedly treats transfers an agent starts as authorized. PNC changed its terms on 18 November, Citizens emailed customers a warning, and Wells Fargo's agreement takes effect on 24 November. The original reporting is credited to TechCrunch, which we did not read directly, and dates vary across outlets.

What to do: don't give general agents your raw bank credentials. Prefer read-only open-banking access. If you build payment agents, require human approval for outgoing money, use scoped and revocable tokens, and tell users about the liability shift.

## 4. RoboBuilder: agents co-design real robots — *research*

Nehme, Nam, Srinivasan and Ahmed tested four frontier coding agents designing both the hardware (standard parts, motors, one custom 3D-printed part) and the control code for ten obstacles at three difficulty levels. The study ran 16,427 simulations of 12,774 distinct robots and built 135 physical robots (about 240 hours of build time).

- 66% of designs that passed in simulation also passed in the real world.
- Using only public part data, none of the first 15 simulation-passing designs crossed the real course.
- When agents saw video of their real failures, they redesigned the robots and sometimes corrected the simulator. This removed 72% of level-1 and level-2 failures.

Takeaway: give agents rich evidence of real-world failures, not just test results.

- [arXiv 2611.23855: RoboBuilder: Agentic Robot Co-Design with Real-world Video Feedback in the Loop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23855)

## 5. "Language Models Make New Research Sound Old" — *research, with a technique to use today*

Guy Zyskind used 40 questions from newly accepted COLM papers that were published after the models were trained. Asked directly, models judged about 92% of them as unstudied (98% for GPT-6-Astra). In ordinary answers, models said plainly that the question was open only about 52% of the time (8% for GPT-6-Astra). Novelty grades barely separated open questions from studied ones. The author notes that models may already know some of the papers being judged.

Takeaway: to check novelty, ask directly whether the specific question has been studied, and by whom. Don't trust numeric novelty scores.

- [arXiv 2611.23546: Language Models Make New Research Sound Old](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23546)
