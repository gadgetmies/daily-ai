# AI round-up, 15 November 2026

Today: llama.cpp 1.0 and new Qwen3.8-27B quantizations, Perplexity's Decider v1.3 open weights, Kiln Harness Tuner, the No AI Slop manifesto, and Anthropic's "Worse Together" study of multi-user agent teams.

## 1. llama.cpp and ggml reach 1.0, plus Unsloth Dynamic V3.1 Qwen3.8-27B quants (actionable today)

- llama.cpp and ggml tagged v1.0.0 on 13 November 2026. They now use semantic versioning: a major version bump for any breaking change to the C API, minor versions for compatible features and new models, and patch versions for fixes only.
- The release removes 35 deprecated functions, including `llama_set_warmup` and `defrag_thold`/`--defrag-thold`, and renames `llama_set_embd_state_src` to `llama_set_ctx_state_src`.
- New: an FP8 KV cache (`-ctk`/`-ctv f8_e4m3`, with optional per-tensor scales), a host-memory MoE expert cache, the NextN speculative-decoding API in the public `llama.h`, and a `/v1/audio/speech` endpoint in llama-server. llama-server is also hardened against SSRF, CORS and DNS-rebinding attacks.
- Unsloth released Dynamic V3.1 GGUFs for Qwen3.8-27B. Unsloth says they are 5–10% more accurate at the same size on Terminal Bench and DeepSWE. These are vendor figures, and one user reported more tool-call errors than with a standard Q6_K quant.

## 2. Perplexity Decider v1.3 and Decider Flash open weights (actionable today; follow-up to decision models)

- Apache-2.0 weights are on Hugging Face, and the models are also available through the Perplexity Decisions API and OpenRouter. Reports date the release to 12 or 13 November.
- Decider v1.3 is built on a Qwen 27B backbone (about 49 GiB in BF16). Flash is distilled to a Qwen3.5-4B base (about 8.5 GiB).
- These are decision models: they return probabilities over options rather than generated text. The new **locate** feature returns exact text spans, for named-entity recognition, PII detection and extractive QA.
- Perplexity reports 91.6% mean accuracy on 63 internal classification tasks (self-reported).
- For agent security, Decider audits samples of recorded agent logs offline, checking for misses where nothing was flagged and for false alarms where something was. Perplexity says one day covered 1B tokens and 7,000 sessions and led to 14 rule fixes, 6 of which shipped within two days.
- One source says OpenRouter is serving both models free until 19 November. This is unverified.

## 3. Kiln Harness Tuner (actionable soon)

- An open-source agentic optimizer that tunes an agent's tools, prompts, skills, model and hyperparameters. Each candidate runs several times (3 by default) on a train split and is promoted only if it also wins on validation. The optimizer never sees the test split.
- It requires the agent to be a Kiln task with evals, with Kiln and provider keys on the same machine. Install with `uv tool install harness-tuner`; `harness-tuner --demo` costs about $2 according to the README.
- Kiln's own claim for one production agent: 2.5x fewer failures, 6x lower cost, 2x lower latency.

## 4. The No AI Slop manifesto (ways of working; follows the 13 Nov fragile-codebase item)

- Published at the AI Engineer Code Summit in San Francisco, modelled on the Agile Manifesto, and presented by Dex Horthy in the closing keynote. People sign it by commenting on the GitHub gist. Horthy reports more than 300 signatures.
- It defines slop as "when we put less effort into producing something than we ask the consumer to spend understanding it."
- It asks signers to own the intent, taste and consequences of what they ship, to measure what shipped and who it helps rather than lines of code, PRs merged, tokens spent or agents run, and to turn recurring mistakes into guardrails such as linters and checks.

## 5. "Worse Together": multi-user, multi-agent teams (research)

- An Anthropic alignment blog post and arXiv paper 2610.00583, from the Anthropic Fellows Program. It compares one coordinator agent serving all users with one agent per user, across four shared-resource environments (an API-key budget, a clinic calendar, personal-assistant bookings and a merge queue), five frontier models from Anthropic, OpenAI and Alibaba, and 77 scenarios.
- The per-user teams often underperformed the single coordinator, though the gap varied with the model, environment and intervention. In the personal-assistant environment, the coordinator fulfilled targeted requests twice as often. Fixes such as adding a team-lead agent worked differently in each environment.
- Three of the environments are being released as MAMUBench.
- A figure of about 58% of team episodes containing unsupported claims about other people appears in only one roundup and is unverified.
